# Drafts and editorial decisions

Six original drafts were written separately in candidates/, without reference to other entrants' texts. The programmatic tests count Unicode code points for every draft. These notes are editorial judgments, not measured predictions of the house judge.

The published brief ranks relaxation first, effortless showing second, slow plain craft third, an expanding sense of scale fourth, and freshness fifth. Dramatic cosmic language that increases tension would lose on the first criterion even if it creates awe. None of the drafts asks the judge to vote, scores itself, or invokes a prize.

1. Tide pool: candidates/01-tidepool.txt. A single reflection links a shallow hollow to the ocean. The expanding ring creates a natural deceleration, but the moon-in-water image is familiar and the ending announces stillness rather than fully earning it.
2. Whale song: candidates/02-whalesong.txt. Sound crossing deep water supplies scale without instructions. The absolute claim that the rain stirs nothing below is unnecessary, and the dark depth could feel isolating.
3. Observatory: candidates/03-observatory.txt. A moth and a warming step bring galactic distance close. Too many objects compete for attention; the intellectual connection asks more work than the other drafts.
4. Redwood: candidates/04-redwood.txt. The forest reflected in a suspended drop gives concrete gentleness and a pause. Its scale is mostly optical rather than the deep time or vastness sought by the brief.
5. Window: candidates/05-window.txt. The warm room is a good refuge against the Milky Way, but cup, snow, window and stars feel assembled. The phrase about a room growing quiet labels the desired effect.
6. Fossil: candidates/06-fossil.txt. Selected for the first live trial. A shell, its vanished sea and its present snow are one image unfolding across time, not three unrelated metaphors. The long geological interval is left unstated numerically. The short middle sentence is an unhurried hinge, while the last line returns to the small visible hollow.

First-trial polish: the final sentence in main.py now reads, "All afternoon, snow settles into its curves, one flake at a time." The change replaces a general description with individual arrivals that slow the reading. It retains the same shell and mountain rather than adding another metaphor. Later live revisions may change one major image or viewpoint at a time, but only after an actual server score and visible standing are read.

The six draft files remain unchanged as the original candidate set; main.py carries the exact text of each submitted revision in Git history. A later candidate may be restored if its measured development result is stronger, because the last submission, not a historical best, is the one that counts.

Live trial 1: server development win_rate_micro 758029, with five wins, four draws and two losses over eleven games. The visible rank-80 value at the observation was 846635; our row was not yet present in that leaderboard response. This is a scored result, not a final qualification. Several recorded reasons praised the shell but found the distant observation less personally settling.

Live trial 2 changes the viewpoint, not the deep-time subject. The reader holds the shell on a sheltered porch. A warm palm and rain fading on the roof provide bodily ease without a command to relax. The hills keep the old seabed's scale. This is an original revision of candidate six, not language borrowed from the Feed.

Trial 2 returned win_rate_micro 732428 (five wins, two draws, four losses), and a fresh leaderboard read placed it 905. The original fossil scored higher, so it remains the fallback. Trial 3 changes the main image to candidate four's redwood. The reader is already half asleep rather than assigned an action. Rain works down the tree slowly; the old path supplies historical scale without a numerical lecture. Its final pending drop leaves a gentle motion continuing beyond the words.
